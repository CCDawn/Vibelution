# Historical AI backtests

`financial_backtest_service.py` is the public facade; the router only parses typed requests and maps domain errors. The existing MA endpoint remains compatible.

The Agent-owned private artifact store is the authority for previews, immutable inputs, accepted decisions and simulation results. It is separate from native Session transcripts and the paper account. Runs never submit broker or paper-account orders and never update Agent memory. Preview reads public historical data without invoking a model. Starting a UUID-idempotent run explicitly authorizes its frozen decision and token limits.

`data.py` fetches bounded yearly raw and qfq windows with warm-up. The default remains Tencent; `dataSource=eastmoney` or `sina` selects a free alternate adapter; `auto` attempts Tencent, Eastmoney, then Sina as independently validated whole snapshots. Sina reads a bounded trailing 1,023 daily bars and strict JSON/JSONP factor data without executing remote JavaScript. It converts share volumes to the shared wire unit without rounding shares. Windows outside provider coverage fail closed. Auto mode also restarts the entire basket with the alternate source; it never splices windows or constituents across providers. Invalid requests and detected corporate actions never trigger that switch. It validates OHLC, volume, dates, ordering, duplicate observations, requested endpoints and in-range dates against the same provider's Shanghai index calendar. Missing sessions are blocked because a source gap cannot be distinguished reliably from a halt. The index is not an official exchange audit source. Raw/qfq quantization intervals retain both feasible additive and multiplicative hypotheses, avoiding false action flags when rounding changes which hypothesis fits. They detect material changes in the adjustment transform; detected corporate actions block the default price-only run; the opt-in cash policy below requires additional official proofs. None of these providers supplies an authoritative corporate-action ledger here, so tiny or complex actions may still escape detection. Data hashes retain reproducibility; a passed factor check must never be described as proof of no corporate action.

`transport.py` reuses the market reader's rate, size and six-second request timeout for historical GETs. Only classified transient failures receive one retry; short `Retry-After` values are honored, while long or invalid cooldowns stop rather than being shortened. Access denial, certificate failures, invalid JSON/response structure, missing qfq and integrity failures never trigger transport retries. Only the explicit `auto` data-source mode can subsequently attempt an independently validated whole snapshot from another public provider. Terminal read errors identify the symbol, exact window, raw/qfq/calendar role and attempts. This mitigates transient transport outages; it cannot repair provider coverage or guarantee end-to-end availability. Model invocation retries remain unchanged and disabled.

`engine.py` owns deterministic long-only, cash-account simulation: versioned board quantity rules, T+1 sellability, completed-close decisions, next-open fills, bounded OHLC slippage and versioned transaction fees. Zero-volume and locked/one-price bars are conservatively rejected. Order records explain no-action and rejection outcomes. Queue priority, exact historical ST/listing regimes and share distributions are not modeled. Cash distributions require the opt-in verified policy below. Partial fills enforce the daily-volume cap, applicable quantity increments and single-order research ceiling. The engine is a research simulation and cannot establish real execution availability.

`fees.py` is the sole historical stamp-duty schedule and owns the opt-in `feeModel=verified_ashare_fees_v1`. Missing or legacy fee models preserve previous parameters, hashes and commission-plus-stamp results. The new model is limited to Shanghai/Shenzhen A shares from 2015-08-01: client commission includes handling and regulatory charges; transfer fees and stamp duty are additional. Included charges are audit allocations, never added to cash deductions a second time. Unsupported markets/windows and commissions outside verified bounds fail closed. Minimum commission applies once per simulated order; half-up rounding to cents is an explicit model convention, not verified broker settlement. The fee version and policy audit bind preview, recovery, forks, rolling windows, comparison and exports; strategy and benchmark use the same rules and affordability checks.

Strategy and buy-and-hold use the same cost/execution rules, daily close marks and initial cash. Terminal positions remain marked to close rather than assuming a sale. Risk/return, fees and trade counts come from deterministic ledger calculations. Price-only runs also expose realized-position win rates; an unclosed position has no realized win rate. Sparse or degenerate series must produce unavailable statistics rather than fabricated Sharpe values.

New previews may opt into `corporateActionPolicy=verified_cash_dividends`; the default remains `block` for compatibility. `official_dividends.py` reads a bounded, issuer-checked CNINFO announcement window and complete implementation PDFs. `cash_action_snapshot.py` requires explicit terminal-page coverage and matches every detected adjustment to the official ex-date and cash amount using rounded raw/qfq OHLC intervals. Unmatched adjustments, ambiguous/revised implementation notices, unsupported share distributions and missing event sessions fail closed. These public checks do not certify exchange-wide corporate-action completeness.

`corporate_actions.py` owns the versioned FIFO cash-dividend ledger. Record-day closing net shares acquire entitlement; ex-date opening recognizes a receivable; payment-day closing makes the dividend available for subsequent trading. Sold entitled shares incur the verified holding-period tax; tax assessed before payment remains payable and is deducted on payment. Equity includes receivables minus assessed tax liabilities; order cash excludes receivables. The strategy and benchmark use the same ledger, including shared-cash baskets and replayed portfolio prefixes. Price-limit reference closes subtract verified cash distributions on ex dates. Terminal unsold positions can incur additional tax upon later sale; settlement-to-cent rounding is unverified, so the ledger preserves exact decimal arithmetic and exposes that limit in its audit. Price-only realized win statistics are unavailable in this mode. Selling with insufficient usable cash for deferred dividend tax fails closed and retains the local order, trade and cash/tax reconciliation in the execution failure artifact; it never finances a negative balance.

Corporate actions, coverage and price proofs participate in the frozen data hash. Runtime, recovery, forks, cost stress and rolling slices remain offline. Every rolling window starts with an independent cash account; pre-window record dates confer no entitlement, while payments beyond window end remain frozen. JSON export schema 4 carries the full snapshot and both account audits; CSV carries daily receivables and tax payables for both accounts, coverage, artifact identity, metrics and audits. Model inputs include only structured events whose publication day is strictly before the decision day; audit/PDF text never becomes instructions. Historical tax periods outside the verified rule interval and bonus/capitalization shares remain unsupported.

`decision.py` invokes the existing configured model through the unified stateless LLM adapter, with no tools, current Agent memory, persona or conversation transcript. Each input consists of a bounded price prefix ending on the decision date, the simulated portfolio at that date, frozen strategy/parameters and the exact output contract. Public `targetWeight` is a percentage from 0 to `maxPositionPct`; the engine receives a ratio after explicit conversion. Invalid JSON, wrong dates, missing reasons, nonfinite numbers and excessive positions stop that step, never silently becoming hold signals. Model training hindsight cannot be eliminated, so results are not prospective accuracy measurements.

The optional `researchInput=announcements` freezes public announcement metadata per stock and decision date during preview: at most three stocks, twenty stock/date pairs and five items per pair. Only publication dates strictly before the decision date qualify, conservatively excluding same-day items without trusted intraday timestamps. Titles and links are untrusted source data, not verified financial values. Coverage warnings remain visible; all-source failure blocks preview. The existing owned store saves a separate immutable research artifact keyed by `researchHash`. Preview reservations include its message bytes, and runtime/fork/resume read only that frozen artifact with hash, symbol and schedule validation. Changing news availability cannot silently change a resumed input. JSON export includes the frozen artifact; CSV includes its identity and coverage summary. Price-only legacy prompts and inputs remain unchanged.

New previews default `maxParticipationPct` to 1% of each asset's observed daily share volume, rounded down to board lots. Strategy and benchmark fills share this cap; partial fills retain requested and actual amounts, and insufficient capacity rejects the order. Strategy remainders are not carried as live orders; the benchmark retains cash after its first partial fill. This daily approximation does not prove opening-auction liquidity or queue availability. Legacy runs without the parameter retain their previous uncapped execution; new caps participate in comparison fingerprints.

The dedicated inference profile caps output at 512 tokens and disables automatic transport retries. Qwen thinking is explicitly disabled without mutating the operator configuration. Other configurations with explicitly enabled reasoning are rejected during preview; intrinsic model reasoning can still consume the cap, and truncated or malformed output requires explicit recovery. Preview, execution and drift checks share the same effective profile. The exported model configuration hash contains no credentials. A separate portfolio margin keeps preview reservations valid as account values change.

`service.py`, `store.py` and `runtime.py` own atomic persistence, cross-process coordination, managed background work, stop/resume, budgets, idempotency and export. An invocation is persisted before calling the provider. A crash or unknown result cannot be automatically resent: the public `paused_unknown` state requires confirmation for the exact decision date and possible duplicate charge. Successful decisions are retained during recovery. The configured model and frozen input hashes remain bound to the run; provider/configuration drift must fail closed. JSON retains provenance and audit records. CSV escapes formula-like text cells.

Lifecycle logs record only stable Agent/run/date identifiers, state, reason codes and counts; model prompts, responses, keys and portfolio contents are not logged. Tests cover accounting, no-future input, malformed decisions, persistent recovery, duplicate starts, scoped reads, HTTP serialization and desktop controls. Runtime and browser acceptance are distinct from unit tests.

## Shared-cash portfolios and held-out windows

Historical requests load intersecting calendar years first, then up to two earlier years only if required for warm-up and a pre-start raw/qfq baseline. All fetched pairs retain the same strict validation; an absent explicit qfq series is never relabeled from raw. The coverage audit is sorted chronologically independent of request order.

`basket_data.py` freezes at most ten canonical A-share symbols as a version-2 snapshot. Each constituent passes the existing coverage, calendar, corporate-action and hash checks; all assets must share exactly the same sessions. Restoration rechecks every asset and the basket digest. Single-stock snapshots remain compatible.

`portfolio_engine.py` uses one cash balance, values all target positions from the same pre-trade account equity, then sells before buying in canonical symbol order. Model targets cover the full basket; each position remains bounded and their sum cannot exceed 100%. The benchmark splits initial cash into equal fixed allocations, starts at the same next-open boundary and retains unfilled allocations as cash with an audit trail. It uses the same lots, commission, historical stamp duty and slippage; it never drops an untradeable constituent. Portfolio-at-date inputs exclude later prices and report same-day purchases as unsellable.

`evaluation.py` divides the continuous account at a date frozen before execution. The held-out period starts from the previous close, with separate starting equity for the strategy and its benchmark. Costs belong to their actual execution window. Window metrics omit realized win rates because a sale may close a lot opened before the window. A held-out result is a retrospective check, not protection against model-training hindsight or proof of future returns.

## Fixed-data candidate runs and comparisons

`robustness.py` adds a read-only descriptive projection of completed, coverage-verified curves: natural years and non-overlapping 63-session windows. Frozen holdout boundaries restrict all segments to the held-out period; absent holdout is explicitly retrospective. Positions carry across boundaries, returns use the previous close, and fees belong to fill dates. Partial tails remain visible but are excluded from the outperformance fraction. Four complete windows are a display threshold, not statistical significance. Annual windows do not certify full-year coverage. Missing or malformed legacy evidence produces an unavailable state. UI, JSON and CSV share this projection; nothing is persisted as a second result authority.

A fork preview restores an owned run's frozen snapshot and keeps its dates, universe, holdout boundary and accounting assumptions. It never reloads current market data. Strategy, eligible configured model and the individual run's token cap may change; the existing explicit start and ordinary run lifecycle still apply. Candidate eligibility and the effective model fingerprint are checked before execution and on resume. This does not modify the Agent's bound model or relax the ordinary preview's bound-model rule.

`comparisons.py` owns comparison provenance and read-only result evaluation. Its contract version binds execution, benchmark, held-out evaluation and decision-prompt semantics; changes to those semantics require a new version. Common-parameter and strategy hashes separate shared assumptions from the intended comparison axis. Two to four owned runs can be compared only when their frozen inputs and shared assumptions match. A strategy comparison requires an identical model fingerprint; a model comparison requires an identical strategy hash. Runs lacking provenance remain readable, but cannot be retrospectively certified. Incomplete and incompatible groups expose their reasons and never produce a winner.

The response is an exportable comparison manifest, not a second result store or worker. Each member retains its own token limit, stop/resume controls and unknown-call confirmation. Model fingerprints identify configured routes and generation settings; they do not prove a provider's underlying weights are immutable.

## Versioned execution and source evidence

`priceLimitModel=supplied_daily_limits_v1` opts into offline imported daily bounds. `dailyPriceLimits` is a JSON array with one version-1 object per canonical stock: `symbol`, `source` (`url`, document `sha256`, UTC `fetchedAt`), and sorted `records` (`date`, decimal-string `upper` / `lower`). Both bounds must explicitly be null for a session without limits. All execution sessions must be present; warm-up sessions need no records. Unknown fields, dates, symbols, mixed nulls, invalid prices, or OHLC outside bounds fail preview before inference. Official-domain URLs and document hashes are supplied provenance, not independently verified documents or historical ST/listing identities. The product does not fetch those URLs or execute source content.

The artifact participates in the price data hash. Strategy and benchmark reject a buy at the imported opening upper bound or a sell at the lower bound, including non-flat bars; this conservative rule cannot establish auction liquidity or queue priority. Replay, recovery, forks and rolling slices keep their frozen bounds without network retrieval. JSON schema 6 includes the self-contained single/basket snapshot; CSV includes every daily bound, artifact/source hashes and the supplied-unverified status. After explicit mode selection, the desktop form accepts files up to 4 MB with at most 5000 sessions per stock. Storage and JSON/CSV exports retain the 8-million-character cap and fail without truncation. Omission or `candidate_bands_v1` keeps legacy hashes and candidate-band execution; imported values never silently activate that mode or enter model prompts.

New previews freeze `quantityRuleVersion=ashare_quantity_v2`. Legacy runs without that field retain their existing 100-share execution, and forks preserve the source version rather than adopting the latest default. Quantity versions enter comparison provenance so results from different quantity contracts cannot be compared as a strategy or parameter experiment. Daily open execution remains an approximation; board quantity rules do not establish auction queue priority or historical price-limit eligibility.

Under the new contract, single-stock close-of-day model inputs exclude shares purchased that day from `sellableShares`, matching the portfolio T+1 projection. Legacy input projection remains unchanged for reproducible replay; actual sell execution occurs on the following session.

`research_inputs.py` dispatches immutable research kinds. Legacy announcement metadata retains its original schema, hash and prompt. `financial_reports` uses official PDF page excerpts, with original-document and excerpt hashes, publication dates and explicit coverage limits. These are source excerpts, not certified structured financial values. Same-day publications are excluded; restoration, forks and exports read owner-local frozen artifacts. A kind mismatch fails closed rather than degrading to price-only decisions. Frequency-changing forks remain prohibited for either research kind because the frozen evidence is tied to its exact decision dates.

## Rolling train/select/test plans

Walk-forward workflows coordinate ordinary backtest runs; they do not call a separate model runner or duplicate transcripts. Preview slices an owned frozen price snapshot and fixes chronological training/test windows, position-cap candidates, score/tie-break rules, model identity and worst-case total token reservation. Preview itself does not invoke a model. Each training candidate starts an independent cash account and makes fresh model decisions; only the selected training winner runs on the corresponding independent test window. Test metrics never enter candidate selection. The last close of each window has no next-open fill inside that window, so its decision is omitted. Test accounts begin in cash and first execute after their first in-window close.

Plans are retrospective walk-forward studies: the user may already have seen the source run, and model training can contain historical knowledge. They must not be presented as blind prospective validation or evidence of future profitability. Public/free daily bars also cannot establish historical ST status, all listing-stage price-limit changes, auction liquidity or complete corporate-action coverage.
